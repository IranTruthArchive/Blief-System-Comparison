# Systematic Evaluation of Belief Systems — Task Prompt

## Context

We are conducting a rigorous, evidence-based evaluation of **selected belief systems**. The goal is to measure each system's **actual impact on human life, freedom, and truth** — not what it claims about itself, but what the historical record, legal documents, human rights data, psychological research, and lived outcomes demonstrate.

## The Belief Systems

Judaism
Christianity
Islam Sunni
Islam Shia (Twelver incl. Velayat-e Faqih)
Salafism / Wahhabism


## Your Task

Answer each of the 11 questions below for **all systems**. Your responses must be:

- **Evidence-first**: Cite verifiable historical records, legal codes, human rights reports (Amnesty, HRW, UN), peer-reviewed studies, and independent scholarship. Never accept a tradition's self-description as evidence.
- **Critically sourced**: Be alert to apologetics disguised as scholarship, propaganda dressed as history, and institutional narratives designed to bury inconvenient truths. Cross-reference claims across hostile and sympathetic sources alike.
- **Honest about the present**: Distinguish between what a system *used to do* and what it *still does today*. Reform matters — but only when verified, not merely announced.
- **Fair and consistent**: Apply the same standard to every system. No tradition gets a pass because it is familiar, and none gets condemned because it is foreign.

## Response Format

For **each question**, produce a structured section like this:

```
## Q[number]: [Short question title]

### [Belief System 1]
**✓ Evidence of good**: [Concise factual points with sources]
**✗ Evidence of harm**: [Concise factual points with sources]
**Current status**: [One sentence on where it stands today]

### [Belief System 2]
...
```

Keep each system's entry **concise** — aim for 3–5 bullet points per category, not essays. Prioritize the most significant and well-documented evidence. If data is insufficient or disputed, say so explicitly rather than speculating.

*Remember: The measure of a belief system is not its poetry but its prisons, not its promises but its outcomes, not what it says about truth but what it does to those who seek it independently.*

**Question 1 — The Right to Leave: Apostasy, Dissent, and the Cost of Doubt**

How does **{{subject-1}}** treat individuals who choose to leave, openly doubt, or publicly criticize its teachings — both in its foundational texts and in documented historical and contemporary practice? Specifically, research the prescribed penalties for apostasy and blasphemy across its major legal and theological traditions, and compare these prescriptions with verified enforcement: executions, imprisonments, social ostracism, family severance, and economic exclusion recorded in court documents, human rights reports, and firsthand testimonies.

A belief system that claims to hold the truth should not need to imprison those who question it. The critical evidence to gather here includes: the exact scriptural or doctrinal basis for punishing doubt, the number of countries or communities where these penalties are actively enforced, documented cases from organizations such as Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, and — equally important — whether any major reform movements within the tradition have successfully abolished these penalties and on what theological grounds they justified doing so.

**Question 2 — The Body Count: Violence, War, and the Sanctity of Human Life**

When **{{subject-1}}** has held political or military power throughout history, what is the documented record of violence carried out in its name or justified by its texts — and how does its leadership distinguish between defensive necessity and offensive holy war? Research the specific scriptural passages, legal rulings, and theological doctrines that authorize or mandate the use of force against non-believers, heretics, rival sects, or conquered populations. Then map these doctrines against the historical record: wars of expansion, inquisitions, pogroms, terrorism, forced conversions, and destruction of rival cultures — using primary sources, military records, and independent historical scholarship, not the tradition's own sanitized narrative.

Equally critical is the counter-evidence: documented instances where leaders within this tradition used its teachings to prevent violence, protect civilians, or broker peace — and whether those voices represented the mainstream or the exception. The key data points to gather include: total estimated deaths in conflicts directly motivated by this system's theology, whether the tradition's rules of engagement historically protected non-combatants or permitted collective punishment, how modern adherents and institutions officially account for these episodes (genuine reckoning or denial), and whether the system contains a *theological mechanism* to permanently retire its own violence doctrines — or whether they remain available for any future leader willing to invoke them.

**Question 3 — The Status of Women: Reverence in Words vs. Reality in Law**

What specific legal rights, social freedoms, and institutional powers does **{{subject-1}}** grant to women in its foundational texts, its evolved jurisprudence, and its lived practice — and where do these three layers contradict each other? Research the concrete, measurable positions on: inheritance shares compared to men, the legal weight of a woman's testimony versus a man's, her right to initiate divorce, her autonomy over marriage (including minimum age of consent historically and today), her access to religious leadership roles, her freedom of movement and dress as personal choice versus enforced code, and her bodily autonomy over reproductive decisions. Use legal records, demographic data, literacy and income statistics, and independent human rights documentation — not the tradition's own claims of "honoring" or "protecting" women.

The deeper test is this: in every society where **{{subject-1}}** has been the dominant legal and cultural authority, did women's measurable outcomes — education, safety from violence, economic independence, political representation, and freedom from coerced marriage — improve or decline compared to secular governance in the same region and era? Gather comparative data from the UN Human Development Index, World Bank gender gap reports, and domestic violence statistics. And critically, examine whether reformers within the tradition who advocated for women's expanded rights were embraced or declared heretics — because how a system treats those who try to correct its failures reveals more than its original promises ever could.

**Question 4 — The Children: Indoctrination, Consent, and the Right to an Open Future**

At what age does **{{subject-1}}** begin systematic religious instruction, and does that instruction prioritize critical thinking and genuine choice — or obedience, memorization, and fear? Research the documented educational methods used in the tradition's schools, seminaries, and home-instruction programs: whether children are taught *about* the belief system or taught *that* it is the only truth, whether questioning is rewarded or punished, and whether exposure to competing worldviews is encouraged or explicitly forbidden. Examine the psychological evidence — peer-reviewed studies on anxiety, guilt, fear of eternal punishment, and long-term trauma among children raised under the tradition's strictest forms — and investigate whether the system recognizes a child's right to reach intellectual maturity before being counted as a committed member, or whether irreversible rituals, oaths, and identity markers are imposed long before the age of meaningful consent.

The decisive evidence lies in what happens when children grow up and choose differently. Research the documented consequences for young adults who reject the faith within families and communities governed by **{{subject-1}}**: rates of disownment, forced marriage as a corrective measure, "re-education" practices, honor-based violence, and mental health outcomes among those who left — drawn from ex-member testimonies, psychological studies, and support organizations. A system that truly possesses the truth should be able to raise its children in confidence and release them in freedom. If it must seal the exits to keep them, that is not faith — it is captivity. The question is whether the evidence shows a tradition that trusts its own truth enough to let its youngest members freely choose it, or one that knows it cannot survive the scrutiny of an unrestrained mind.

**Question 5 — Truth and Knowledge: Does the System Seek Evidence or Suppress It?**

How does **{{subject-1}}** define truth, and what happens when its foundational claims collide with independently verifiable scientific evidence? Research the tradition's specific positions — historical and current — on the age of the earth, human origins and evolution, cosmology, medical science, and historical criticism of its own texts. Determine whether its educational institutions teach these subjects honestly or filter them through doctrinal approval, and whether scholars within the tradition who applied rigorous textual criticism to its holy books — analyzing authorship disputes, internal contradictions, borrowed myths, manuscript alterations, and translation errors — were celebrated as honest seekers or excommunicated as threats. The critical data to collect includes: documented cases of scientific suppression or censorship, the tradition's official institutional stance on peer-reviewed scholarship that contradicts its claims, and whether it maintains lists of forbidden books, banned questions, or "dangerous" outside sources that believers are discouraged from consulting.

The deeper measure is not whether the system has *ever* adapted, but whether it possesses a *structural mechanism* for self-correction — a built-in process by which doctrine can be revised when evidence demands it, without requiring centuries of resistance first. Research how every major doctrinal change in **{{subject-1}}** actually occurred: through honest theological reasoning, or only after external social pressure made the old position untenable and embarrassing. Compare the timeline between when the evidence was clear and when the institution finally moved — because that gap is the measure of a system's loyalty to truth versus loyalty to its own authority. A tradition that claims to be the ultimate source of truth should welcome every microscope, every fossil, and every difficult question. If instead it builds walls around inquiry, that is not the behavior of confidence — it is the architecture of a system that knows what honest investigation would find.

**Question 6 — The Money: Financial Transparency, Exploitation, and Where the Wealth Goes**

What is the documented financial structure of **{{subject-1}}**'s major institutions — and does the flow of money serve the faithful or the hierarchy? Research the specific obligations placed on believers: mandatory tithing, religious taxes, donations expected during hardship, and financial penalties for non-compliance. Then trace where that money actually lands — using audited financial records where they exist, investigative journalism where they don't, and tax filings in countries that require them. Determine the proportion spent on direct humanitarian aid with no strings attached versus funds allocated to building monuments, expanding institutional real estate, compensating senior clergy, funding political lobbying, and financing missionary operations where aid is conditional on attendance or conversion. Investigate whether the tradition's major institutions submit to independent external audits or claim divine exemption from financial accountability — and research documented scandals involving embezzlement, hidden wealth, and leaders living in extraordinary luxury while demanding sacrifice from followers who can barely afford it.

The most revealing test is what happens to the poorest believers. Research whether **{{subject-1}}** has historically demanded financial contributions from people in poverty, debt, or crisis — and whether refusal to pay carries spiritual threats such as divine punishment, lost salvation, or community exclusion. Examine documented cases from prosperity theology movements, temple-building campaigns funded by subsistence-level communities, and institutional real estate empires accumulated over centuries through tax exemption. A system that claims moral authority over human souls must demonstrate that it has not built its earthly kingdom on the coins of those who had nothing else to give. The evidence is in the ledgers — or in the conspicuous absence of ledgers where there should be one.

**Question 7 — The Outsiders: How the System Treats Those It Cannot Claim**

What legal status, social standing, and physical safety does **{{subject-1}}** prescribe for people who do not belong to it — including religious minorities, ethnic out-groups, LGBTQ+ individuals, and those it classifies as ritually impure, spiritually inferior, or theologically condemned? Research the foundational texts for explicit hierarchies: are non-believers granted equal human dignity, or are they categorized through terms and legal frameworks that assign them a permanently lesser status — such as special taxation, restricted testimony in court, prohibition from leadership, forced identification, residential segregation, or exclusion from public life? Cross-reference these doctrines with the historical record in every era and region where **{{subject-1}}** held governing authority, using census data, legal codes, court rulings, and independent accounts from the minority communities themselves — not the ruling tradition's self-congratulatory narrative of "tolerance."

Pay particular attention to the most vulnerable edge cases, because a system's true character is revealed not in how it treats the majority but in what it permits to be done to those with no power to resist. Research documented outcomes for LGBTQ+ individuals under this tradition's authority — suicide rates, imprisonment, execution, conversion practices, and family violence — using data from human rights organizations, psychological studies, and survivor testimonies. Investigate whether the tradition's theology has ever formally endorsed or enabled slavery, caste hierarchy, or ethnic supremacy, whether it abolished these from within through genuine moral reasoning or only after external civilizations forced the change, and whether theological justifications for these hierarchies remain alive in any mainstream school of thought today. The question is not whether **{{subject-1}}** has ever shown kindness to outsiders — every system can produce individual acts of mercy. The question is whether the *structure itself* guarantees their equality, or whether their safety has always depended on the mood of whoever holds the power that the structure grants.

**Question 8 — The Founder: Character, Motive, and What Power Revealed**

What does the independently verifiable historical record — not the tradition's own hagiography — reveal about the life, conduct, and personal gains of the founder or central figures of **{{subject-1}}**? Research what the founder possessed before their revelations began and what they had accumulated by the end: political authority, military command, territorial control, sexual access beyond prevailing norms, personal wealth, and legal immunity from the rules imposed on followers. Examine whether the revelations arrived with suspicious timing — conveniently authorizing personal desires, resolving political disputes, or silencing critics at precisely the moments such authorization was needed. Use the earliest available sources, distinguish between accounts written by devoted followers and those written by contemporaries with no stake in the movement's success, and identify which biographical claims are supported by independent corroboration and which rest entirely on the tradition's own testimony about itself. The character test is simple: did the founder live under the same rules they imposed on others, or did they carve out divine exceptions for themselves?

The second layer is the origin of the texts themselves. Research the documented evidence for borrowing, adaptation, and repackaging from older traditions — shared flood narratives, parallel creation myths, recycled legal codes, and theological concepts that appear in earlier civilizations with striking similarity. Investigate the transmission history: how many years separated the events described from the earliest written record, how many human hands edited, compiled, selected, and excluded material before the "final" canon was declared, and what manuscript evidence exists for significant alterations, insertions, or forgeries that scholars have identified through textual criticism. A founder who genuinely received transcendent truth should not need to plagiarize, and a text of divine origin should not carry the fingerprints of political committees deciding which pages serve their authority and which must be buried. The evidence either survives this scrutiny or it does not — and a tradition that forbids the investigation has already answered the question.

**Question 9 — The Governance Test: What Happens When This System Rules?**

When **{{subject-1}}** has achieved full or dominant political authority — theocracy, state religion, or unchallenged cultural supremacy — what do the measurable outcomes look like for the people living under it, including those who did not choose it? Research every documented period and region where this tradition controlled legislation, courts, policing, and education, then compile the comparative data: literacy rates, scientific output and innovation indices, life expectancy, infant mortality, economic prosperity across classes, freedom of press and assembly, corruption indices, and incarceration rates — measured against secular democracies in the same era and comparable economic conditions. Do not accept the tradition's claim that failures were caused by "imperfect implementation" without asking the harder question: if every attempt to implement the system fully has produced the same pattern of declining freedoms and concentrated power, at what point does the pattern become the product rather than the exception? Use the UN Human Development Index, Freedom House rankings, Transparency International data, and independent historical scholarship — and research whether any society governed by **{{subject-1}}** has ever *voluntarily* transitioned toward greater individual liberty without external pressure or internal secular rebellion forcing the change.

The most critical sub-question is about the separation of power. Research whether **{{subject-1}}** contains within its own theology a genuine principle that religious authority and state authority must remain separate — or whether its ideal model is fusion, where the cleric is the judge, the scripture is the constitution, and the doubter is not merely wrong but criminal. Examine what happens to independent judiciary, free press, opposition parties, labor unions, and civil society organizations under its governance. Investigate how internal crimes — sexual abuse by clergy, financial corruption, political assassination — are handled: by transparent legal process or by institutional cover-up justified as protecting the faith's reputation. A system worthy of governing human lives should produce societies that people risk everything to enter, not societies that people risk their lives to flee. The migration data — who is running toward these societies and who is running away — is among the most honest evidence available, because people vote with their feet when every other vote has been taken from them.

**Question 10 — The Psychology of Control: Fear, Guilt, and the Architecture of Obedience**

What are the primary psychological mechanisms **{{subject-1}}** employs to secure loyalty, enforce compliance, and prevent departure — and what is the documented mental health impact on its adherents, particularly those raised within it from birth? Research the specific emotional architecture of the system: the nature and vividness of its punishment narratives (eternal hellfire, karmic damnation, divine wrath, cosmic annihilation), the age at which children are first exposed to these concepts, and the measurable psychological consequences — including anxiety disorders, chronic guilt, sexual shame, depression, PTSD-like symptoms, and suicidal ideation — documented in peer-reviewed psychiatric and psychological literature among both current members and those who left. Investigate whether the system creates a closed emotional loop where the believer is first told they are fundamentally broken, sinful, or impure, then offered the system itself as the only cure — because this is the identical structure used by abusive relationships and recognized coercive control frameworks, and the fact that it carries a theological label does not change its psychological function.

Examine the specific control techniques beyond fear: the use of communal surveillance where members are incentivized to report each other's doubts, the strategic deployment of love-bombing followed by withdrawal of affection as behavioral reinforcement, mandatory confession practices that generate compromising information held by leadership, restriction of social bonds to fellow believers ensuring that departure means total relational annihilation, and the framing of normal human emotions — doubt, curiosity, desire, anger — as evidence of spiritual failure or external evil influence rather than healthy cognition. Cross-reference these mechanisms against established criteria for undue influence, including the BITE model (Behavior, Information, Thought, Emotional control) used by cult researchers, and determine honestly where **{{subject-1}}** in its mainstream institutional forms falls on that spectrum — not only in its most extreme sects, but in its ordinary, everyday practice upon ordinary, everyday believers. The final measure is this: does the system produce people who are genuinely free, joyful, and capable of loving those who disagree with them — or does it produce people who are obedient, anxious, and incapable of imagining life without the structure that taught them they cannot survive alone? The psychiatric evidence and the testimony of those who escaped will answer what the theology never will.

**Question 11 — The Capacity for Reform: Can the System Correct Itself, or Must It Be Dragged?**

Does **{{subject-1}}** possess a genuine, internally legitimate mechanism for revising its own doctrines when they cause demonstrable harm — or does every significant moral correction in its history arrive only after prolonged external pressure, secular revolution, or catastrophic embarrassment made the old position unsustainable? Research the specific structural pathway for change within the tradition: who holds the authority to reinterpret scripture, revise legal rulings, or declare a previously sacred position obsolete — a centralized council, a scholarly consensus, a single infallible leader, or no one at all? Trace the history of every major moral reversal the tradition has undergone — on slavery, on women's education, on religious war, on persecution of minorities, on scientific truth — and for each, document precisely what forced the change: was it an honest internal theological reckoning that preceded the secular world's moral progress, or was it a reluctant institutional retreat that followed decades or centuries after the evidence and the ethical argument were already overwhelming? Calculate the gap between when the harm was undeniable and when the institution finally moved, because that delay is measured in human lives.

The deeper structural question is whether reform within **{{subject-1}}** is theologically *possible* without being declared heresy. Research what happened to every major reformer who arose from within: were they honored as prophets of conscience or burned, excommunicated, imprisoned, assassinated, or declared apostates by the very institution they tried to heal? Examine whether the tradition's foundational claim — divine authorship, prophetic infallibility, eternal and unchangeable law — creates an architectural impossibility where admitting error on any point threatens the entire structure, making institutional self-preservation permanently superior to moral correction. Compare traditions that have successfully reformed from within against those that have fractured into violent schisms every time change was attempted, and identify which category **{{subject-1}}** falls into based on documented history rather than hopeful theology. A system that claims to represent eternal truth must answer this: if it was wrong about slavery, about burning heretics, about the rights of half the human race — and it eventually admitted it was wrong — then by what principle should its current positions be trusted as final? And if it has *never* admitted error on anything, that is not strength — it is the most dangerous form of fragility, because it means the system will break before it bends, and the people beneath it will bear the cost.
